## Reading the numbers

- **Physics changed almost nothing.** The servos added nothing, as the spec predicted. The pipeline lag, though, was 40 ms, not the UR5e's ~240 ms the spec assumed:
  - The sim gravity-compensates every body, so there's no sag.
  - The upstream STS3215 gains settle in about 70 ms, with about 1 ms of tracking lag.
  - The arm runs at the sim's default speed limit of 180°/s with no acceleration limit, twice the loop's 90°/s.

  So the measured 40 ms lag (two 20 ms ticks of streaming pipeline) is the same as the kinematic sim's. The servo dynamics add nothing measurable on top.
- **Why the UR5e lagged 240 ms is now an open question.** Its sim arm was configured with `max_joint_deg_s 60` and `max_joint_deg_s2 120`, which the SO-101 runs don't set. The acceleration limit is the likely cause of the UR5e's long pipeline lag, its larger tracking error and its queue-cut stop, but that's a hypothesis, untested here. What 3b does show is that **the loop itself adds about 2 ticks of lag. Anything beyond that is on the arm side (the sim arm's config or queue), most likely the acceleration limit.**
- **The ramp-stop decelerates** (5.5 mm against the ideal ≈ 5 mm), as it did on the kinematic SO-101, because there's no long queue for `arm.Stop` to cut.
- **What 3b does validate over 3a:** the SVA/MJCF agreement for the real SO-101 geometry, to 0.05 mm under `validate`. It also shows the loop's results hold under physical joint motion with force limits, friction and contacts: everything converged, and the jaw stayed clear of the table with the 100 mm riser.

- **Settling wasn't measured separately.** Every static run exits as soon as the error is under 2 mm, so the 1.92–1.99 mm values are the convergence threshold, not a steady-state error.

## Finding fixed during the runs

- **`cli validate` failed at first:** FK 0.133 mm and angular Jacobian 13%.
- **Cause:** it read the joint angles and the end pose in two calls straight after `MoveToJointPositions` returned. A physics arm is still settling at that point, within its 0.5° path tolerance, so the two reads disagreed. A 1° nudge then compared a moved pose against stale joints.
- **Fix (`e547cee`):** read the joints, then the end pose, then the joints again, and accept the sample only once the joints didn't move in between. After the fix, every bar passes.
- **Hardware follow-up (final review):** a Feetech servo holding position can flicker ±1 encoder count (≈ 1.5e-3 rad) indefinitely, so a timeout no longer fails. It keeps the stillest sample and prints a warning. For 3c, a one-count mismatch already costs about 0.45 mm at 300 mm reach, so the 0.1 mm FK bar may need revisiting on hardware.
- **Impact:** only `validate` was affected, and the kinematic sims never showed it. It matters for 3c, because hardware also settles after a move returns.

**Operational note:** `validate` samples poses around wherever the arm is. A rerun started from a low pose left by the bench drove samples into the table: "arm did not reach target", with the shoulder stopped about 4° short. Start `validate` from home (`q0a`). Rerun from there after the timeout change, it still passes everything: FK 0.054 mm / 0.019°, Jacobian 3.0% / 1.2%.

## Starting values for 3c (sim-derived)

None of the sims model the real STS3215 servos or the Feetech bus, so these are starting points only:

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `singular_eps` / `max_damping` | 0.03 / 0.05 | From the SO-101 kinematics sweep (3a); unchanged by physics |
| `resync_deg` | default (3 ticks at max joint speed) | 0 resyncs with a 40 ms pipeline; revisit if the bus adds lag |
| Loop joint limit | 90°/s | Clean at 50 Hz; the real STS3215 no-load speed is higher |
| Rate | 50 Hz | Holds in every sim run; the Feetech bus rate is the brief's open risk |

Hardware has to re-derive the lag, the settling behaviour and the stop shape, including whether the driver queues waypoints the way the MuJoCo UR5e config did.
